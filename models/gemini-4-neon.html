What Gemini 4 Neon costs

$3 per 1M input and $15 per 1M output, with cache reads at $0.15 — a 95% discount on the input rate for repeated prefixes. The 1.05M-token window bills at one flat rate with no long-context tier, unlike Gemini 3.1 Pro's 200K break.

Neon lands between the promotional Carbon ($2/$10, rising to $4/$20 on 2026-12-01) and Hydrogen ($1.50/$7.50). Priced on delivered task cost rather than headline tokens, it is the family's value pick for agent workloads.

Neon, Carbon or Hydrogen

Hydrogen is the cheap volume tier at $1.50/$7.50, Carbon the premium reasoning tier, and Neon the middle. Google positions Neon for complex AI tasks where you want flagship-adjacent judgement without flagship spend.

The practical rule: route simple classification and extraction to Hydrogen, agent and coding loops with a real failure cost to Neon, and reserve Carbon for the small slice where the depth of reasoning is the deciding factor.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Gemini 4 Neon cost per 1M tokens?
$3.00 per 1M input tokens and $15.00 per 1M output tokens, with cached input at $0.15 per 1M — 5% of the input rate. The free tier covers limited requests on both AI Studio and the Gemini API.
How does Neon compare with Gemini 4 Carbon?
Carbon lists at $2/$10 (promo) and Neon at $3/$15 (standard). Independent tests measured Neon's per-task cost at roughly 17% below Carbon's with ~40% shorter completion time, which is the whole pitch: a mid-tier model that undercuts the flagship on delivered tasks.
Is Gemini 4 Neon a reasoning model?
Neon targets complex, multi-step AI tasks and scores 61 on Artificial Analysis' intelligence index — third globally, ahead of its price tier. Google routes it for agents and long workflows rather than simple chat completions.
