How long should a password be?

Entropy — not length alone — is what matters. Randomly drawn from 62 alphanumeric characters, each character adds about 5.95 bits of entropy. A random 12-character password is roughly 71 bits; 20 characters is 119 bits, which is beyond anything brute-forceable. What actually breaks accounts in practice is reuse and phishing, so: unique random password per service, stored in a manager, plus a second factor.

Passphrases trade raw entropy for memorability: 4 random words from a 1,800-word list give about 42 bits, while 6 words give about 63 bits. Diceware-style lists are designed so every word carries full entropy and no word is embarrassing.

Frequently asked questions

Is the randomness good enough for credentials?
Yes. The tool uses crypto.getRandomValues(), a cryptographic pseudo-random generator (the same primitive used to generate TLS keys), not Math.random(), which is predictable and unsuitable for secrets.
Where are the passwords generated?
Entirely in your browser, on your device. Nothing is transmitted, logged or stored — closing the tab destroys the values, so copy them before you do.
What does "exclude look-alikes" do?
It removes characters that are easy to confuse when reading a password aloud or from a screenshot: l I 1 | O 0 o and similar pairs.
