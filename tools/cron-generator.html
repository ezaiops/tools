    How cron expressions are built
    
    
    A cron line has five fields — minute, hour, day of month, month, day of week — separated by spaces. Each field accepts a wildcard (*), a single value, a list (1,15), a range (1-5) or a step (*/5). This builder emits the shortest correct form for whatever you pick, so 0 9 * * 1-5 — 9am on weekdays — stays minimal instead of expanding into lists.
    
    
    One classic trap: when both day-of-month and day-of-week are restricted, cron fires on days matching either field, not both. If you pick "the 1st" plus "Monday", you get the 1st and every Monday — usually not what people intend. Leave one of the two on "Every day" for the intersection you expect.
    

    
    Frequently asked questions
    
    
    
      Can I build a six-field expression with seconds?
      
    No — this builder targets standard Unix five-field cron. Quartz-style expressions with a seconds field are a different syntax used by Java schedulers; translate them by hand.
    
    
    
    
    
      Why does my weekday schedule also fire on the 1st?
      
    Because you restricted both the day-of-month and day-of-week fields. Cron ORs them together. Set day-of-month back to "Every day" and the weekday restriction will apply alone.
    
    
    
    
    
      Is the generated expression valid on Linux and macOS?
      
    Yes — both use Vixie cron, the same dialect this builder targets. Systemd timers and Kubernetes CronJobs also accept standard expressions.
