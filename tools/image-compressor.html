What this tool actually does

Your file is read with FileReader into an <img>, drawn onto a <canvas> (which is where resizing happens), and re-encoded with canvas.toBlob() at the quality you chose. That is the same encode/decode path the browser itself uses — there is no magic, and no server.

  • WebP typically gives 25–40% smaller files than JPEG at equivalent quality, and supports transparency that JPEG cannot. Supported by all current browsers.
  • JPEG is the universal fallback; quality 80 is the sweet spot for photos.
  • PNG is lossless — re-encoding an already-PNG file rarely shrinks it unless you resize or drop to 8-bit colors, which this tool does not do.

Best results come from starting with the largest original you have: downscaling in canvas beats upscaling a small file, and a photo shot at 4000px compressed to 25% usually looks better than a 1000px original at 25%.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a file size limit?
The practical limit is your device's memory. Files up to roughly 20–30 MB work fine on desktop; very large images (100 MB+) may fail on phones.
Does quality 80 look bad?
For photographs, rarely noticeable. Drop to 60–70 for thumbnails and social previews; keep 90+ for images with text or fine line art.
Where do my images go?
Nowhere. The file picker, canvas and download all happen locally; the page makes no network requests with your image data.
