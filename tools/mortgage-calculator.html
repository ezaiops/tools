How the payment is computed

The standard amortization formula: M = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), where P is the principal, r the monthly rate (annual ÷ 12) and n the number of payments. Each payment covers that month's interest first, with the remainder reducing principal — which is why the balance falls slowly at first and then faster.

An extra payment is pure principal, so it compounds: $200/month extra on a $400k / 30-year / 6.5% loan saves roughly $90k of interest and retires the loan about 7 years early. But only do this after higher-rate debt is cleared and an emergency fund exists — liquidity beats a few points of interest saved.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include taxes, insurance or PMI?
No — this is principal and interest only, the P&I part of a mortgage payment. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA fees and PMI are added on top by your lender.
Are the numbers exact?
Yes for fixed-rate loans — the formula is exact and rounding is applied only to the displayed cents. Variable-rate loans require a schedule per rate period.
Can I use this for car loans or student loans?
Absolutely — any fixed-rate installment loan works. Just enter the amount, rate and term in years.
