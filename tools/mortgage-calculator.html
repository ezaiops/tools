Equal installment vs equal principal

Equal installment (等额本息) uses the standard amortization formula M = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1): P is the principal, r the monthly rate (annual ÷ 12), n the number of payments. Every payment is identical; each one covers that month's interest first and shrinks the balance slowly at first, then faster. Total interest is higher than equal principal, but the early payments are lower — easier on cash flow.

Equal principal (等额本金) pays a fixed P ÷ n of principal every month plus interest on whatever balance remains. The first payment is the largest (full principal slice plus interest on the whole loan); each subsequent payment is smaller. Total interest is lower — often 15–25% less — because the balance drops immediately, but you need the income to absorb the higher early payments.

Which is better? Equal principal wins if your income comfortably covers the early payments and you plan to hold the loan long-term. Equal installment wins if cash flow is tight or you expect to invest the difference at a return above the loan rate. An extra payment is pure principal in both methods, so it compounds either way.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include taxes, insurance or PMI?
No — this is principal and interest only, the P&I part of a mortgage payment. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA fees and PMI are added on top by your lender.
Which method saves more money?
Equal principal, almost always — its total interest is typically 15–25% lower because the outstanding balance falls from day one. The cost is a higher first payment: roughly 1.3–1.5× the equal-installment payment on a 30-year loan. Run both here with the same numbers and compare the total-interest figures directly.
Are the numbers exact?
Yes for fixed-rate loans — the formulas are exact and rounding is applied only to the displayed cents. Variable-rate loans require a schedule per rate period.
Can I use this for car loans or student loans?
Absolutely — any fixed-rate installment loan works. Just enter the amount, rate and term in years. (Some Chinese auto loans use equal principal with a shorter term; both methods are supported.)
Does this handle China's housing provident fund (公积金) and combined loans?
Yes, in the Chinese version of this page. Pick 公积金贷款 for a single fund loan, or 组合贷 to enter the fund and commercial portions separately — each with its own rate and term. The amortization table then lists fund principal and interest beside commercial principal and interest for every payment, because the two portions amortize independently. Extra payments are applied to the commercial portion first, since it carries the higher rate.
